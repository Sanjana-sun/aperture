Aperture

A layer between you and the platforms. It shows you what your files reveal before you upload them, audits what a platform already holds about you, and drafts the legal requests to get it deleted.

Nothing leaves this device. There is no server, no upload, no analytics, and no network request of any kind. Open your browser's network tab and watch: it stays empty while you use this. Load the page once and it works offline. 0 dependencies.

Private messages

Deliberately not built yet.

The third pillar is real end-to-end encrypted messaging between people who both install this. It is not here, and shipping it quickly would be the wrong call.

Hand-rolled cryptographic protocols fail in ways that only formal analysis catches, and the failures are silent. The plan is to use a reviewed implementation — libsignal (X3DH plus Double Ratchet) or MLS (RFC 9420) — rather than to write a ratchet from scratch.

The same reasoning rules out the approach people usually suggest first: encrypt everything before upload. It fails structurally, not incidentally. Platforms have to render content, they re-encode images and destroy anything embedded in them, and key distribution kills adoption before the crypto ever matters. Keybase and Scramble! both died on exactly this. So Aperture works on what survives that constraint instead of fighting it.

What this does not claim

  • Pattern matching is not certification. The text scanner finds things that look like identifiers. It will miss things, and it will flag things that are harmless. It reduces accidental disclosure; it does not make a caption safe.
  • Metadata removal is not anonymity. Stripping EXIF removes what is attached to the file. The image content still shows where you were, and the platform still sees your account, your IP and your upload time.
  • Adversarial perturbation is not implemented here, and when it is, it will raise an attacker's cost rather than guarantee protection. Platform recompression degrades it, and published defences get broken by later models.
  • Deletion requests are not deletion. This drafts the letters. Whether a company complies, and what exemptions it claims, is outside anything software can control.
  • This never automates anything on a platform. No posting, no scraping, no logging in on your behalf. That is a deliberate design constraint, chosen to stay on the right side of terms-of-service and Computer Fraud and Abuse Act exposure. You click send, always.
